Finding small road damage in unfamiliar countries

Investigate how one road-image detector handles unfamiliar appearance and controlled variation in the boxes used to label damage.

One shared problem. Three independently assessable research contributions.

Candidate proposal · novelty still needs validation
01 / THE PROBLEM

A detector learns from both images and annotations

Appearance can change

Camera viewpoint, road texture, lighting and damage size can differ between the training images and a new country.

Boxes can describe damage differently

A thin crack can be enclosed tightly, loosely, or with a slightly shifted box. That choice can affect training assignments and evaluation overlap.

Evidence boundary: cross-country generalisation is an established research problem. We have not established that RDD2022 contains noisy annotations. Box shifts and changes in size would be controlled experimental perturbations.
02 / WHY SMALL DAMAGE IS SENSITIVE

The same box shift can have very different effects

Move an alternative annotation by the same number of image pixels in both examples. If a prediction exactly matched the reference annotation, this is the overlap it would receive against the alternative.

IoU = intersection area ÷ union area. A value of 1 means identical boxes; 0 means no overlap.

Research implication: test whether learning can be less sensitive to plausible box variation while preserving genuine localisation accuracy. Improving overlap by simply predicting larger boxes would not establish better damage detection.
03 / THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Study a specific failure, rather than a new detector demo

Can annotation-tolerant assignment and small-damage training preserve recall in an unseen country, without using that country's labels or adapting to its images?

What existing work already covers

Cross-scene adaptation, multi-source training and road-damage domain generalisation already have recent methods, including MSDA-RDD and MAMDG.2,3

What this candidate would investigate

The additional value of box-tolerant learning, small-region consistency and source-only confidence calibration under a controlled country holdout.

The methodological distinction still needs checking against the nearest papers. Swapping architectures or choosing another country alone does not establish novelty.

06 / WHAT IS READY, AND WHAT REMAINS

A plausible proposal that still needs a feasibility pilot

Existing data and a bounded implementation

RDD2022 provides road images, training boxes and four challenge classes: longitudinal crack, transverse crack, alligator crack and pothole.1

Start with a compact detector and selected country archives. Demonstrate the result in an offline image viewer with boxes and scores.

Planning estimates: occasional 8–12 GB GPU, 16 GB RAM and 10–25 GB storage. No training time, peak memory or benchmark performance has been measured.
