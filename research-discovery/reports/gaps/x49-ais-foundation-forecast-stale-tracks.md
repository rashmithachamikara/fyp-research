# How reliable is a vessel position forecast when the latest AIS reports are missing?

**X49 · Foundation models for AIS trajectory forecasting · Pilot first · 5 November 2026**

A port traffic analyst replaying a vessel track needs to know how far its position may have diverged since its last Automatic Identification System (AIS) report. A forecast can look accurate on average while its uncertainty circle is too small for vessels that have just begun turning. The proposed test is whether a compact pretrained forecaster can give a **smaller ten-minute endpoint uncertainty circle at comparable observed coverage** than dead reckoning or a locally trained neural model when the last few minutes of history are withheld in a second region. Future AIS positions supply the reference. This is an offline position-estimation study.

## What the literature already answers

| Closest evidence | Consequence for the gap |
|---|---|
| The GeoDiffusion manuscript describes a maritime foundation model for irregular global AIS trajectories, including forecasting, filling gaps and transfer to unseen distributions. The indexed manuscript and supplement describe a grid-based task. [Manuscript](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=w7xpNeFIbb) · [Supplement](https://openreview.net/attachment?id=w7xpNeFIbb&name=supplementary_material) | Handling irregular AIS with a pretrained model is established prior work. Its metric scale, test masks and checkpoint availability need direct comparison before claiming a gain over it. |
| EnvShip already has vessel-disjoint tracks, cross-region forecasting, dead reckoning and trained neural baselines. Its short track uses ten minutes of observations and ten minutes of targets. Sections 4 and 6 and Appendix B describe the metrics, transfer tests and baselines. [EnvShip v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15240v2) | A new regional leaderboard would duplicate much of this experiment. Its shared windows make a *paired* test of the same vessel with fresh and stale histories possible. |
| Published maritime work already calibrates circular position bounds from Euclidean residuals at each horizon. Separate work tests foundation models under contiguous missing observations and measures interval coverage on industrial streams. [Maritime prediction boundaries, methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11902398/) · [Foundation-model degradation study](https://doi.org/10.3390/fi18100550) | Both the calibration method and the missing-block test have precedents. The unresolved comparison in these sources is how much pretrained AIS or general time-series representations buy over **identically calibrated** cheap forecasts under joint staleness and region shift. |

**Hypothesis.** An adapter using time since the last report and recent turning rate will reduce endpoint error or sharpen a 90% circle at similar coverage on stale, turning tracks. **Rival explanation.** Most apparent benefit comes from accounting for elapsed time or making circles wider. Giving dead reckoning and the neural baseline the same calibration data separates these effects. The existing [X31 reception-drift study](x31-ais-reception-drift.md) estimates why reports may be absent. X49 measures position error conditional on a known reporting delay.

## Data, weights and fair comparison

- **Data.** Start with trajectory columns from EnvShip Track A, Denmark for development and a US subset for transfer. The release documents 150,000 Danish and 60,000 US benchmark windows, vessel and segment identifiers, timestamps and relative coordinates. The data card lists Danish public-sector reuse conditions and US public-domain upstream data. Skip the map rasters and their separate licence. Record the exact shards, terms and attribution. [Release data card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mark000071/envship_v2_datasets/blob/main/DATA_CARD.md)
- **Foundation-model route.** Use the public Apache-2.0 Chronos-Bolt-Tiny checkpoint on the east and north displacement series separately, then fit a small two-coordinate residual head. Its coordinate quantiles do not give joint geographic coverage. Include CheminTF as an AIS-pretrained comparison if its listed tutorial weights load and its spatial encoding and cadence cover both regions. Its authors describe it as an educational model. GeoDiffusion weights and compatible resolution remain unverified. An experiment with Chronos alone supports a claim about a general time-series foundation model adapted to AIS. [Chronos model card](https://huggingface.co/amazon/chronos-bolt-tiny) · [CheminTF author repository](https://github.com/GaspardMerten/CheminTF)
- **Leakage control.** Fix the forecast issue time and target timestamp. On paired copies hide the last 0, 2 or 5 minutes of a ten-minute history. Re-anchor every feature to the last *visible* point, remove hidden speed/course and project forward for the full elapsed interval. Inspect how the benchmark interpolated input and reference points. If interpolation across the cutoff or the original coordinate anchor reveals a withheld point, rebuild those windows from raw reports or exclude them. Describe the manipulated gaps as simulated and the benchmark endpoints as resampled. Natural outages lack positions inside the outage.
- **Splits.** Use earlier source dates for fitting, distinct later source vessels for selection and calibration, then later test dates. Keep the US test untouched. Check vessel identifiers across regions, remove overlapping voyages before windowing, and report counts after intersecting time and vessel rules. Check checkpoint training dates and geography separately. Unknown exposure restricts any claim that a region was unseen during pretraining.

## Three assessable contributions on the same test

| Member | Controlled experiment | Result to defend |
|---|---|---|
| A — value of pretraining | Compare unchanged pretrained forecasts, the residual head, the same head with dead-reckoning inputs, and a compact temporal convolutional network trained on the same vessel budgets. Include a random-weight backbone control and match input channels and tuning budgets. | Ten-minute endpoint error and average path displacement in metres versus number of training vessels. |
| B — sensitivity to stale messages | Compare ordinary training, trailing-block augmentation, and an age-aware correction on identical clean/masked tracks. Match the number of missing points with an internal-gap control. Define turning strata from visible history. | Paired increase in endpoint error by gap and motion regime, plus the effect of adding age to each baseline. |
| C — transferable uncertainty | Fit pooled and age-conditioned residual radii on source calibration vessels for every predictor. Compare empirical 90% coverage and median circle area by region and gap, with fixed-radius and dead-reckoning controls. | Coverage and area together, with paired vessel-bootstrap intervals. Regional transfer makes coverage an empirical question. |

**Module decision.** This is the better first pilot. Recorded positions provide targets without annotation, all three studies share one forecast, and the fallback result is concrete: identify the gap lengths and manoeuvres for which a cheap calibrated extrapolator suffices. A prototype can replay an anonymised track with its forecast circle. The modality is time series, which would require the team to look beyond its computer-vision preference.

**Before shortlisting.** In a two-week pilot, read one trajectory shard per region, audit interpolation and identifier/date overlap, run dead reckoning and one released checkpoint on exactly the same windows, and measure disk, memory and time. Start with at most 5,000 windows per region on CPU or one shared GPU. This is a workload cap, not a measured runtime. Proceed if both regions retain independent vessels in each primary gap condition, the issue-time leakage check passes, and the projected three-member comparisons fit 6–8 months. If only one region survives, the regional transfer question needs redesign. Novelty, final data suitability and resource feasibility are open gates, so the [module rubric](../../../research-module-and-team-criteria.md) score is withheld.

**Review scope.** Targeted primary-source check for maritime foundation models, cross-region AIS forecasting, missing-history robustness and conformal vessel-position regions. The remaining gap is an inference across these studies. No checkpoint or dataset shard was run.
