# Vessel movement behaviour from coastal radar tracks without AIS at inference

**ID:** X47 · **Area:** Maritime radar, time-series ML, sensor transfer · **Status:** Conditional research opportunity, subject to a real-track pilot  
**Evidence checked:** 4 November 2026. Links point to source records or papers. This is a proposal appraisal, not an implemented or validated result.

## 1. What the project would do

The input is a time-ordered radar track: positions and timestamps for a detected vessel, plus other radar tracks where an interaction is tested. **The supplied v3 proposal's core output** is the current motion state in a sliding window: transit, stopped/drifting or loitering. It treats an **encounter as a separate two-vessel event**, not a fourth single-vessel class. Turns, approaches, and zone crossings are possible later extensions if the data support them; they are **not** the core v3 claim. AIS can supply example tracks and provisional labels during development, but **the deployed classifier must receive radar-derived data only**. It should report uncertainty when the track is too short, broken, or ambiguous.

The proposed chain is: (1) define operational movement labels; (2) learn from plentiful AIS trajectories with those provisional labels; (3) model the measurement errors, missing detections and track breaks of actual radar; (4) adapt/train a compact temporal classifier; (5) test it on independently held-out real radar tracks. Radar-to-AIS association may establish reference trajectories for **evaluation**, but the associated AIS cannot enter the test model's inputs.

This does **not** identify a vessel's intention, fishing activity, cargo transfer, illegality, or reason for turning. It identifies motion that could warrant further investigation. A vessel without AIS could be classified at inference if radar tracks it; its true activity cannot be validated solely by the absence of AIS.

## 2. Scope of labels and observability

| Candidate output | Operational definition to preregister | Minimum evidence and common confuser |
|---|---|---|
| Transit | Sustained displacement with fairly stable course and speed | Requires a segment, not one radar return; a short leg of a manoeuvre can look like transit. |
| Stopped / slow movement | Speed below a chosen threshold over a duration | Cannot tell anchored, drifting, waiting or sensor jitter apart without more evidence. |
| Turning / course change (**extension**) | Sustained change of heading or curvature | Position jitter can create false turns; specify smoothing and window length. |
| Local repeated movement / loitering pattern | Low net displacement relative to path length, over a specified interval | Long observation is needed. A short port track is insufficient for hours-long loitering. |
| Encounter (**separate pair event**) | Two simultaneously tracked vessels satisfy a prespecified proximity, speed and duration condition | Requires reliable two-track association and time alignment; port traffic creates chance proximity. Global Fishing Watch's 500 m, two-hour, under-2-knot rule is an example for open-sea AIS analysis, **not a threshold to copy blindly into short port radar windows**. |
| Zone entry (**extension**) | Track crosses a predefined geospatial boundary | Boundary geometry must be available and radar coordinates calibrated; entry is not an offence. |

Use mutually compatible **multi-label** events where needed: a vessel can turn while entering a zone. Define an `insufficient evidence / unknown` state. Split events by window length; do not imply that seconds of data can verify an hours-long activity. Vessel *type* classification is a different task.

## 3. Closest prior work and overlap

| Study | What it actually studied | Overlap and remaining question |
|---|---|---|
| [Cope et al., *Coastal radar as a tool for continuous and fine-scale monitoring of vessel activities*, PLOS ONE (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9286260/) | Marine Monitor coastal radar tracks were classified with **heuristic rules** into focal and linear movement patterns associated with potential fishing. | Direct evidence that movement-pattern classification from radar is already done. The proposed contribution cannot be “first radar behaviour classifier.” It could test learned, multi-class AIS-to-radar transfer and measured uncertainty. The paper's patterns do not independently prove fishing. |
| [Jochumsen et al., recursive random forest for 2D radar tracks, *EURASIP JASP* (2016)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13634-016-0378-3) | Classifies real and simulated radar tracks using kinematics and a stationary-target filter; reports 79.7% overall classification on real data. | Direct radar-track ML precedent. Its classes distinguish **target categories/stationarity**, not transit versus loitering behaviour for vessels. The v3 proposal's comparison should use its robust-feature idea, not cite its accuracy as behaviour-classification accuracy. |
| [Bakkegaard et al., AIS kinematics to later radar target classification, IRS (2018)](https://doi.org/10.23919/IRS.2018.8448118) | Uses an RNN trained on labelled Danish AIS vessel tracks as a proof of concept for eventual 2D radar target classification. | The broad “train on AIS for radar” concept is already published. The cited abstract does not establish a real-radar behaviour-transfer test. This paper classifies target/vessel categories, not the v3 motion-state task. |
| [Gamage et al., *Detecting anomalous behaviors in maritime vessel groups from radar images*, *Ocean Engineering* (2026)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0029801826009054) | CeBiPA combines group-motion prediction and LSTM anomaly detection using land-based navigational radar, with 41 real and 61 simulated vessels. | Strong recent radar-based ML precedent. Its task is **group anomaly detection**, not the proposed set of operational movement labels; its evaluation does not by itself establish AIS-to-radar transfer for any vessel. Public training-data access needs checking. |
| [Coro et al., navigation radar detector fishing-area mapping, *International Journal of Digital Earth* (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1080/17538947.2026.2617004) | Estimates fishing **areas and effort** from detected ship navigation-radar transmissions, with or without vessel identifiers, and compares largely with AIS-derived fishing maps. | Important non-AIS precedent but its unit of inference is an area, not a shore-radar track of one vessel. The validation reference is itself AIS-based. |
| [Tao et al., sparse AIS loitering analysis, *IEEE Access* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3637519) | Reconstructs fragmented AIS sequences and classifies loitering patterns. | A strong sparse-track/loitering baseline concept. It does not measure transfer from AIS to a real radar sensor. |
| [Ribeiro et al., *Real-World Marine Radar Datasets for Evaluating Target Tracking Methods*, *Sensors* (2021)](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/21/14/4641) | Benchmarks radar target detection and tracking against AIS reference trajectories. | Demonstrates the radar-to-track and association problem. Its target is tracking accuracy, not movement-event classification. |
| [Pitsikalis et al., *Composite Event Recognition for Maritime Monitoring*, DEBS (2019)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03078) · [published DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3328905.3329762) | A rule-based event recognition system assigns composite event labels to AIS trajectories with spatial context. | Source of **weak training labels** and event definitions. A model trained and tested against the same rules only learns those rules; this is not independent confirmation of real-world activity. |
| [Roughan et al., *Classifying Maritime Vessel Behaviour From AIS* (2026)](https://roughan.info/papers/AustDM_Paper_ShipMotionClassification_cameraready.pdf) | Classifies short fixed-length AIS track segments from movement summaries. | Reinforces that AIS track behaviour classification is established. The open question is measured transfer to real radar-only tracks. |
| [Marine Monitor developer note on ML for radar data (2026)](https://www.m2marinemonitor.com/about/media/m2-blog-we-have-radar-data-now-what/) | Describes planned transit, stopped and manoeuvring classifiers. | Relevant operational direction, but a developer note is **not a peer-reviewed validated model** or open benchmark. |

**Novelty verdict:** Moderate only if the study establishes a reproducible **real radar test** and quantifies what AIS-trained labels/models retain under radar tracking errors. Earlier radar intent/threat work and AIS-for-later-radar classifiers mean the broad concept is old; “first radar behaviour classifier” or “first AIS-to-radar idea” would be false. Standard domain adaptation (for example, DANN), random jitter, or a new classifier name alone are not a sufficient gap. A focused result about **which behaviours remain observable, and when the classifier should abstain** could be useful even if simple rules win. The v3 review did not find an exact real-radar transfer comparison, but that search is not proof none exists; defence proceedings deserve a further hand-check.

## 4. Candidate datasets and exact roles

| Dataset / source | Available content and value | Gap / access risk |
|---|---|---|
| [HAXR, DLR Zenodo record (2026)](https://zenodo.org/records/19824555) | Public multi-station X-band port radar: raw polar radar cells in HDF5 and co-timed anonymised AIS positions in CSV, from 13 partly overlapping stations. Same stable pseudonymous vessel ID is used within the record. Most promising **real radar transfer/test** source. | **Not a labelled radar-track behaviour dataset.** Detection, tracking and association must be constructed or adapted. Available hourly chunks cover a limited recording period; class prevalence and track duration have not been audited. A loitering/encounter class may be absent or too rare. Station copies of one vessel must not leak across splits. |
| [Moreira et al., background subtraction on complex multi-radar data, MARESEC (2026)](https://zenodo.org/records/20810176) | Published preprocessing/tracking work on the multi-radar dataset; a candidate starting point for turning radar cells into usable detections. | A processing method, not supplied behaviour labels or guaranteed continuous vessel tracks. Reproduce on a small HAXR subset before relying on it. |
| [Composite Maritime Events, Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/2557290) · [event-recognition paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.03078) | About 4 million rule-recognised events on six months of semantically annotated AIS around Brest, involving about 5,000 vessels. Useful to study event definitions, class balance and weak supervision. | **Not paired with HAXR radar.** Its event records cannot simply be transferred as truth for HAXR vessels. Expert spot-checking reported in the paper supports only selected precision checks; it does not provide complete independent ground truth or recall for all classes. |
| [US Marine Cadastre AIS](https://marinecadastre.gov/ais/) | Large public AIS position histories usable for extra trajectory pretraining, segmentation and controlled radar-error simulation. | AIS only; scale/cleaning burden. No radar-only validation. Check download, usage terms and geographical bias before committing. |
| [Radar recordings with AIS sidecars](https://github.com/keesverruijt/radar-recordings) | Small practical radar PCAP/video recordings with AIS for selected windows; could test preprocessing and timing assumptions. | Heterogeneous hobby hardware and no behaviour benchmark; likely a fallback engineering sanity check, not a primary evaluation corpus. |

**Important data distinction:** HAXR's AIS and radar are co-timed, which can support matching and held-out reference checks. The Composite Maritime Events labels are from a different place and period. To evaluate HAXR, define labels on HAXR's own reference AIS tracks (or independently adjudicated radar tracks if genuinely available), then withhold every AIS-derived quantity from the model at inference. If the reference label itself is rule-generated, call the score **agreement with an AIS-derived operational label**, not verified intent or verified fishing.

### What the Composite Maritime Events validation actually shows

The [peer-reviewed DEBS paper](https://cer.iit.demokritos.gr/publications/papers/2019/pitsikalis-CERMM.pdf) did use expert feedback, but only for a **selected set of recognized events in the first month**, not a fully labelled six-month test set. Its Table 5 reports 3,067 true positives and 4 false positives for `anchoredOrMoored`, and 52 true positives and 2 false positives for `rendezVous`, alongside three other checked classes. It did **not** establish recall because the complete set of missed events was unknown; loitering was not in that expert-feedback table. Thus the event corpus is stronger than an entirely unchecked rule dump, but its labels are still weak references for the proposed new classes and sensors. A held-out model score against those labels is not evidence of actual ship-to-ship transfer or intent.

## 5. Method and discriminating experiments

1. **Data audit first.** Download a few HAXR station-hours. Confirm radar cell decoding, vessel detection, track length, AIS matching quality, and counts of each candidate event. Inspect whether the radar contains enough examples of stop, turn, approach and zone entry. This is a go/no-go gate, not a formality.
2. **Label definitions.** Use speed, curvature, displacement/path-length ratio, relative range and boundary crossing on AIS only to create weak target events. Keep thresholds and minimum window durations fixed before test analysis. Manually inspect visual examples for gross rule errors, but do not claim independent truth from self-inspection.
3. **Radar tracks.** Convert detections into tracklets, retaining timestamps and quality flags. Record fragmentation, missed detections, duplicate tracks, position error and false alarms against AIS-associated cases. Evaluate tracking separately so classifier failure is not hidden by association failure.
4. **Training inputs.** Make **HAXR's co-timed AIS for the same vessels and hours** the primary source domain, and the associated HAXR radar tracks the target domain. This isolates sensor shift from geography/fleet shift. Danish or US AIS can be optional extra pretraining, with any resulting region shift measured separately. Start with speed, acceleration, turning and displacement features; render radar-like perturbations using the **measured** error distribution. Train a compact tree model and a small sequence model. Domain adaptation is optional and must be compared with direct training and simple augmentation. Labels must never be features.
5. **Real transfer.** Train on AIS-derived data or AIS-associated training partitions; test on distinct HAXR radar tracks. Use AIS solely after prediction to generate the agreed reference and diagnostic matches. Include an end-to-end test from raw radar if feasible and an oracle-track test to separate tracking from classification.
6. **Calibration.** Estimate class confidence on held-out radar data. Test an abstention rule for short, noisy and out-of-distribution tracks; report both coverage and error among predictions made.

**Baselines:** **the same label rules applied directly to raw and smoothed radar tracks**; logistic regression/random forest or LightGBM on track summaries; a simple temporal model; AIS-only training without radar simulation; generic Gaussian jitter; measured sensor-specific corruption; domain-adapted model. All comparisons need the **same vessels, hours, windows and split**. The key ablation is whether radar-specific simulation or adaptation improves **real radar** performance. If a classifier uses only position/time tracks, test whether costly raw-radar processing adds information beyond a competent tracker.

**Domain adaptation, in plain terms:** AIS tracks and radar tracks describe the same vessel motion but have different measurement distributions. AIS positions may be clean and irregularly broadcast; radar tracks may jitter, vanish, merge or fragment. Domain adaptation tries to learn movement features that still work when the input source changes. One option is to train on labelled AIS-derived segments while aligning their internal features with unlabelled radar segments; [CoDATS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.10996) is a general time-series example, **not a published solution to this exact vessel task**. This is useful only if it improves a held-out real-radar test over transparent alternatives such as measured error augmentation. Adversarial alignment can also erase behaviour information if the AIS and radar class mixtures differ, so class-wise results are essential.

**What is synthetic here:** the starting trajectories can be *real* AIS recordings. The synthetic part is the radar-like corruption or observation process placed on top of those trajectories. A full synthetic radar image generator is not necessary for the track-classification question. If one is built, its extra value must be measured separately.

**Error limits versus an error model:** Marine-radar performance standards can supply stress-test bounds, but they are **not** observations of HAXR's actual range/bearing error, clutter, missed scans or fragmented IDs. Fit the primary corruption parameters from radar–AIS residuals and track continuity on training-only HAXR partitions; leave final-test vessels/hours untouched. The [AdaTime benchmark/code](https://github.com/emadeldeen24/AdaTime) can provide reproducible generic adaptation baselines, but its success on other sensors does not predict a maritime result.

## 6. Evaluation and claim limits

- **Split:** group by unique vessel, contiguous episode, day and station where possible. Overlapping stations and adjacent windows cannot put essentially the same passage in both train and test. Prefer at least one held-out station/time slice, and report distribution shift.
- **Metrics:** per-class precision, recall and F1; macro-F1; PR-AUC for rare events; **false alarms per radar-hour** for encounter/event detection; confusion matrix; event detection delay; calibration error/Brier score; risk–coverage curve with abstention. Bootstrap by vessel or episode rather than individual radar returns, repeat runs, and report intervals when the real test is small.
- **Tracking diagnostics:** detection recall, association/ID switches, track fragmentation and position error on matched AIS cases. Provide classifier results with reference-quality tracks and real extracted tracks.
- **Leakage checks:** no AIS speed, identity, vessel type, route, destination or future trajectory at test input; no event label copied from the training-rule output as a test feature; no duplicate multi-station passages across partitions.
- **Robustness:** vary radar miss rate, jitter, clutter, track length, sampling interval and encounter density. Break down errors for small/slow vessels, turns near port channels and crossing pairs.
- **Interpretation:** success against HAXR AIS-derived labels supports radar-only prediction of **AIS-defined movement patterns for matched vessels**. HAXR's AIS files do not provide vessel length, so do not claim a proven **small-boat** result unless vessel size is separately established. It does not validate non-AIS vessels' real activities. A separate, independently labelled non-AIS test would be required for that stronger claim.

If HAXR lacks enough duration or examples, narrow first to the supported core states (for example, transit versus stopped) and publish the observability limit. Do not inflate the class list by manufacturing positive radar cases or by treating simulated radar as independent validation.

## 7. Three connected research contributions

| Member | Own question | Experiment and assessable result |
|---|---|---|
| A — radar measurement and track quality | Which real radar errors most damage behaviour recognition? | Quantify HAXR radar-versus-AIS tracking errors; test measured corruption against generic jitter; report a sensor-error model and ablation. |
| B — AIS-to-radar representation | Can an AIS-trained classifier transfer to held-out radar tracks? | Compare rules, feature ML, small temporal model and a bounded adaptation method on the **same** split; report per-class transfer and compute. |
| C — reliable event decisions | When should a radar-only model decline to classify? | Calibrate outputs, model duration/quality-conditioned uncertainty, compare abstention baselines, and report risk–coverage and failure patterns. |

These are research investigations with independent baselines, not merely dataset, model and frontend chores. An offline track viewer could demonstrate predictions and errors; its UI is not the research contribution.

## 8. Feasibility decision and immediate gates

**Conditional go for a narrow radar-track question; no-go for a broad claim of intent or illegality.** Data and compute are plausible for a three-student, 6–8 month implementation with a modest GPU or CPU feature baselines. The **dominant risk is real, usable radar event coverage**, followed by extracting accurate tracks from HAXR cells. The supplied v3 proposal calls for a **two- to three-week pass/fail pilot before commitment**.

**Resource envelope:** Start with a small subset of HAXR station-hours, AIS feature extraction and CPU rules/tree models. Store compact derived tracks rather than repeatedly reading full radar-cell volumes. A single modest GPU is enough for a small sequence model or bounded adaptation run; a large radar-image model is an optional escalation. Storage and runtime depend on the HAXR subset and decoder and have not been benchmarked. This route is generally lighter than training a full satellite-video detector plus classifier.

1. Obtain HAXR sample files and confirm the licence, decoder, station timestamps and AIS association feasibility.
2. Count candidate positive events and complete track durations **after** radar extraction, by train/test group. Define minimum sample counts before proceeding.
3. Reproduce simple radar rules and a simple AIS-trained feature model. Require a held-out real-radar result before adding domain adaptation.
4. If the pilot fails, report a narrower track-quality/observability study or select another project; AIS-only classification is already studied and would not answer the stated question.

**Source status:** The linked dataset records and primary papers support the descriptive claims above. Exact HAXR event prevalence, SAT-independent activity truth, model performance, licence suitability for a derived release and compute budget have **not** been measured here.
